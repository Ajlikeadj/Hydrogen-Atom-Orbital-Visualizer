Radial Distribution & Wavefunction

The curve shows the probability of finding the electron at a distance r from the nucleus per unit radial distance. Switch to R(r) to inspect the raw signed radial wavefunction amplitude and zero-crossing nodes, or Both to compare both curves simultaneously. Vertical dashed lines mark radial nodes where P(r) = 0. The red marker shows the most probable radius, the global maximum of P(r). Switch on Cumulative to add the running total P(r ≤ R) on the right-hand axis — it rises from 0 to 1 and shows how much of the electron's probability lies inside a given sphere.

Wavefunction Cross-Section

A true slice through the wavefunction: blue means ψ > 0, pink means ψ < 0, and dark regions are close to zero. The sign flips across every node, which is exactly what makes bonding and antibonding combinations possible. The colour ramp is compressed towards zero (the numbers on the bar are still exact) so that faint outer shells stay visible next to the bright central peak. The most informative plane is selected automatically for each orbital; some planes are pure nodal planes where ψ vanishes identically (for example xz for the 3dxy orbital), and the plot says so when you pick one.
